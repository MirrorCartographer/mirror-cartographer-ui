What this experiment is testing

Adding evaluators can reduce independent random error. It cannot automatically remove a bias shared by the whole panel. When evaluators inherit the same flawed rubric, framing, training signal, or missing context, their errors may be correlated.

The simulation makes that mechanism visible. It does not claim that real evaluators have these exact probabilities; the controls are illustrative assumptions, not estimates from a field study.

The practical implication: do not treat high agreement as proof of validity. Add independent reference evidence, adversarial cases, subgroup analysis, rubric audits, and an adjudication path.

Deterministic model structure; stochastic trials. A fixed seed reproduces the same run. No external data, tracking, or network requests. A synthetic experiment from the Mirror Cartographer Human Judgment Lab. The source and assumptions are part of the exhibit; no real-person evaluation data is used.
