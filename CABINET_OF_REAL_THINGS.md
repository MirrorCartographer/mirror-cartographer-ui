# The Cabinet of Real Things
*What you can enter, read, or build from*

This cabinet is a reminder that the repository is not only a promise about a future interface. There are already distinct artifacts with their own shapes. Some are playable experiments; some are production plans; some are story structures. Their status matters.

## Matchbox Weather — an independent interactive
[**Open the source**](independent-creative-web/matchbox-weather/index.html)

A self-contained canvas-based weather experience with a paper-and-ember palette, a small central stage, and a deliberate transition from label to atmosphere. The neighboring files include weather-score experiments and source-contract tests.

This is the strongest first stop when you want to *enter a piece* rather than read a description of the entire project. The source exists; if an interaction behaves unexpectedly in your environment, record that as a concrete defect instead of assuming the intended experience succeeded.

## Make a Door Out of Thunder — a production-locked film package
[**Read the production package**](media/thunder-door/MAKE_A_DOOR_OUT_OF_THUNDER_V2_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE.md)

A little girl learns to stitch lightning into a door—not to escape fear, but to transform the room that held it.

The package defines a 90–120 second handmade stop-motion illusion, a dark illustrated room, gold thread, a ghost, a portal, and an ending in which the room changes. It also makes a strong creative decision: technical systems should appear as magic behavior, not visible interface jargon.

This is a production package, not proof that the final film has been completed. The story and visual direction are already specific enough to guide production.

## The Room-World — an experiential interface
[**Active entry point**](src/main.jsx) · [**Room-World component**](src/components/RoomWorldValid.jsx)

The room-world is designed as a sensory-first encounter rather than a dashboard. Its visual and audio logic belongs to the larger vocabulary of weather, memory, phrase contours, rhythm, and changing atmosphere.

A source file is not the same thing as a successful live experience. Treat a working local interaction, a successful build, and a verified deployment as three different kinds of evidence.

## The Living Atlas — the larger field
[**Read the atlas source**](public/atlas/index.html)

The atlas is meant to connect independent pieces, research, tools, games, and the paths between them. It is not supposed to reduce the repository to a list of folders.

The useful test for every atlas entry is simple: does the link lead somewhere real, does the destination explain itself, and can a person return without losing the thread?

## The Between — relationships as an instrument
[**Read the source**](research-atlas/index.html)

The Between explores how context, path, and point of view change a relationship. It should let a visitor inspect a link rather than merely admire a web of connections.

A relationship is a candidate for inquiry, not automatic proof of causation.

## The House That Forgets Gravity — a separate creative world
[**Read its identity contract in the research repository**](https://github.com/MirrorCartographer/MirrorCartographer/blob/main/independent-creative/the-house-that-forgets-gravity/IDENTITY.md)

A room changes its physical law in response to the visitor's choice. Domestic objects become characters; motion expresses the altered premise. The work is intentionally independent of Mirror Cartographer's terminology and systems layer.

## The recurring result

Across these artifacts, the work keeps asking how an invisible change can become perceptible:

- weather becomes an environment;
- fear becomes a thread and a door;
- gravity becomes a rule the room must obey;
- a relationship becomes a path you can reverse;
- memory becomes a trace that can be followed again.

That is the creative result worth protecting: **an abstract structure becomes an experience without having to lose its complexity.**

## The next honest step

Do not add more doors just to make the map look large. Inspect the existing ones. Repair broken routes, finish the most promising incomplete piece, and record the difference between what was imagined, what was built, and what was actually verified.

A small world that truly responds is worth more than a hundred doors painted on a wall.
