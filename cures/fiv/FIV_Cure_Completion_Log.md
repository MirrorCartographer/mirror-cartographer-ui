# FIV Cure + Prevention Completion Log

Status: literal execution log for cure discovery, O'Malley protection, and prevention in FIV-negative cats  
Subject: Feline Immunodeficiency Virus (FIV)  
Created: 2026-07-01  
Linked map: `cures/fiv/FIV_Cure_Map.md`

## Purpose

This log exists because “FIV has no current cure” is not the endpoint. It is the wall. The task is to map how the wall would be broken while protecting the living cat now.

Every recommended test, procedure, research method, prevention method, or intervention must be logged with:

- Name.
- Purpose.
- Why it matters.
- Literal completion steps.
- Who can perform it.
- Required sample/data.
- Result meanings.
- Result limits.
- Risks/failure modes.
- Next action triggered.
- Status.

## Non-negotiable safety boundary

This is not a DIY veterinary treatment plan. It is a research and clinical-navigation log. Any procedure, drug, anesthesia, biopsy, imaging, antiviral, immune therapy, vaccine decision, trial enrollment, or experimental treatment must go through a licensed veterinarian or legitimate research protocol.

The living cat comes first. Cure discovery cannot delay breathing assessment, lymph-node workup, pain relief, infection treatment, nutrition, hydration, or emergency care.

# Track 1: O'Malley living-cat protection

## 1.1 Resting respiratory rate log

Purpose: Detect breathing instability early.

Why: Enlarged lymph nodes, anemia, thoracic disease, infection, heart/lung disease, pain, or lymphoma can affect breathing. Oxygenation outranks abstract cure research.

Literal completion:

1. Wait until O'Malley is asleep or deeply resting.
2. Count one inhale plus one exhale as one breath.
3. Count for 30 seconds and multiply by 2, or count for 60 seconds.
4. Log date, time, body position, recent activity, heat/stress level, and breaths per minute.
5. Repeat daily if breathing is a concern, otherwise several times weekly.
6. Record video if breathing looks effortful.

Who: Owner at home; vet interprets concerning trends.

Data: Breaths/minute, posture, effort, gum color, video if abnormal.

Meaning:

- Stable, relaxed breathing is reassuring but not curative proof.
- Rising rate, increased effort, open-mouth breathing, pale/blue gums, collapse, or inability to rest is urgent/emergency.

Limits: Normal breathing does not rule out lymphoma, FIV progression, anemia, infection, or internal lymph-node disease.

Risk: Counting while awake, hot, purring, moving, or stressed can mislead.

Next action: Emergency vet if distress; routine vet review if trend worsens.

Status: Recommended now.

## 1.2 Lymph-node anatomical map

Purpose: Convert “swollen nodes” into specific evidence.

Why: FIV can cause lymphadenopathy, but lymphoma, infection, and inflammatory disease can look similar. Cure discovery fails if everything is lazily labeled “just FIV.”

Literal completion:

1. Ask vet to identify each enlarged node by name: mandibular, prescapular, axillary, inguinal, popliteal, mesenteric, thoracic, or other.
2. Measure each node in centimeters or millimeters.
3. Record symmetry, firmness, mobility, pain, warmth, fixation, and growth rate.
4. Photograph or diagram external swelling.
5. Recheck on a defined interval.
6. Mark any node affecting swallowing, breathing, walking, or comfort.

Who: Vet for formal map; owner for trend observation.

Data: Node location, size, texture, mobility, pain, trend.

Meaning:

- Mild symmetric enlargement may be reactive.
- Large, firm, asymmetric, fixed, painful, rapidly growing, or breathing-related nodes require sampling/imaging.

Limits: Palpation alone cannot rule out lymphoma.

Risk: Owner palpation can be inaccurate or stressful.

Next action: FNA/cytology for suspicious nodes.

Status: Recommended now.

## 1.3 Lymph-node FNA/cytology

Purpose: Separate reactive enlargement from lymphoma, infection, or other pathology.

Why: If O'Malley has lymphoma or a treatable infection, FIV cure research does not replace that. If nodes are reactive, that also changes the map.

Literal completion:

1. Vet selects the most abnormal accessible node.
2. Small needle aspirates cells.
3. Slides are prepared and reviewed, preferably by a clinical pathologist.
4. If unclear, ask about flow cytometry, PARR/clonality testing, core biopsy, excisional biopsy, or imaging-guided sampling.
5. Record exact cytology language, not only “good/bad.”

Who: Veterinarian; pathology lab.

Sample: Lymph-node aspirate.

Meaning:

- Reactive: immune activation/inflammation likely, cause still needed.
- Suppurative/pyogranulomatous: infectious/inflammatory track.
- Monomorphic/atypical lymphocytes: lymphoma concern.
- Non-diagnostic: sample failure, not proof of health.

Limits: FNA can miss lymphoma or mixed disease.

Risks: Mild bleeding, stress, non-diagnostic sample; sedation may be needed for some cats.

Next action: Treat infection/inflammation if found; oncology workup if lymphoma suspected; repeat/biopsy if inconclusive.

Status: Recommended now.

## 1.4 CBC with differential

Purpose: Measure immune, anemia, platelet, and infection/inflammation clues.

Why: FIV-positive cats can have cytopenias, secondary infections, anemia, inflammation, or marrow effects. AZT-like drugs also require CBC monitoring if ever considered.

Literal completion:

1. Vet collects blood.
2. Request CBC with manual smear review if abnormalities appear.
3. Record WBC, neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, RBC, hematocrit, hemoglobin, platelets, and smear comments.
4. Compare against prior labs.

Who: Vet/lab.

Sample: Blood.

Meaning:

- Anemia: bleeding, hemolysis, chronic disease, marrow disease, parasites, kidney disease, cancer.
- Low neutrophils/lymphocytes: immune/marrow concern.
- High WBC: infection/inflammation/stress/cancer possibility.
- Platelet issues: bleeding/clotting risk.

Limits: CBC does not diagnose FIV cure status.

Risks: Blood draw stress/bruising.

Next action: Abnormal values trigger targeted testing, imaging, infection workup, or oncology evaluation.

Status: Recommended now.

## 1.5 Chemistry panel + urinalysis

Purpose: Check organ reserve before any procedure or treatment.

Why: Kidney/liver/protein status changes what procedures, anesthesia, drugs, or trials are safe.

Literal completion:

1. Vet collects blood and urine.
2. Chemistry: kidney values, liver values, proteins/globulins, electrolytes, glucose.
3. Urinalysis: specific gravity, protein, sediment, infection clues.
4. Ask about urine protein:creatinine ratio if protein loss is suspected.

Who: Vet/lab.

Sample: Blood and urine.

Meaning:

- High globulins: chronic immune stimulation/inflammation.
- Kidney/liver abnormalities: treatment constraints.
- Proteinuria: kidney/inflammatory disease.

Limits: Normal values do not rule out lymphoma or active FIV reservoir.

Risks: Urine collection method may require cystocentesis.

Next action: Treat/monitor organ issues; use results before sedation/anesthesia or major drugs.

Status: Recommended now.

## 1.6 Thoracic imaging if breathing is affected

Purpose: Detect chest lymph nodes, lung disease, fluid, masses, heart enlargement, or structural compression.

Why: Breathing concern plus lymph-node enlargement raises stakes.

Literal completion:

1. Ask vet whether chest X-rays are sufficient or ultrasound/CT is needed.
2. Image before assuming anxiety or “just FIV.”
3. Record whether lungs, heart silhouette, mediastinum, pleural space, and thoracic lymph nodes look abnormal.
4. If sedation is needed, evaluate risk first.

Who: Vet/radiologist.

Data: Radiographs/ultrasound/CT report.

Meaning:

- Chest mass/nodes/fluid: urgent diagnostic path.
- Normal X-ray: reassuring but not proof of no disease.

Limits: Small lesions may be missed.

Risks: Stress, restraint, sedation if needed.

Next action: Sampling, oncology, infection workup, or respiratory treatment depending on findings.

Status: Recommended if breathing is truly affected.

# Track 2: FIV status, strain, and baseline

## 2.1 Confirm FIV/FeLV status

Purpose: Verify infection status and co-infection risk.

Why: FeLV changes prognosis, transmission, cancer risk, and household prevention. FIV antibody tests can be affected by vaccination history in some contexts.

Literal completion:

1. Review prior FIV/FeLV test type and date.
2. Repeat FIV/FeLV SNAP or equivalent if history is uncertain.
3. If results conflict, ask about confirmatory testing such as Western blot, PCR, or lab-based assays.
4. Record whether cat was ever given FIV vaccine.

Who: Vet/lab.

Sample: Blood.

Meaning:

- FIV positive: exposed/infected depending on assay context.
- FeLV positive: separate high-impact disease layer.
- Discordant: needs confirmatory path.

Limits: Test interpretation depends on age, vaccine history, assay, and timing.

Risks: Blood draw stress.

Next action: Confirm before mixing cats or making prognosis claims.

Status: Recommended if documentation is incomplete.

## 2.2 FIV viral sequencing / subtype identification

Purpose: Identify the viral target for future cure design.

Why: Cure discovery needs sequence data. CRISPR, vaccines, antibodies, and entry studies fail if they target variable regions.

Literal completion:

1. Ask whether a veterinary diagnostic lab, university, or research collaborator can sequence FIV from blood/lymph tissue.
2. Collect sample under vet/research protocol.
3. Sequence env, gag, pol, LTR, and regulatory regions if possible.
4. Compare sequence to known FIV subtypes/clades.
5. Identify conserved and variable regions.

Who: University/research lab; vet coordinates sample.

Sample: Blood, PBMCs, plasma, lymph-node sample, or stored diagnostic sample.

Meaning:

- Subtype/strain known: enables targeted research.
- High variability: demands multiplex targets.
- Conserved sites: candidate cure targets.

Limits: Sequencing virus does not treat cat.

Risks: Privacy/sample handling; blood draw; possible cost.

Next action: Use sequence for guide-RNA, antibody, vaccine, and reservoir research design.

Status: Research track.

# Track 3: Reservoir map

## 3.1 Identify reservoir cell types

Purpose: Find where replication-competent FIV persists.

Why: You cannot cure what you cannot locate. Delivery must match reservoir anatomy.

Literal completion:

1. Review FIV reservoir literature: CD4+ T cells, CD8+ subsets, B cells, monocytes/macrophages, lymphoid tissue, and other tissue sites.
2. In research setting, isolate PBMCs and lymph-node cells from infected cats.
3. Use flow cytometry to sort cell populations.
4. Measure cell-associated FIV DNA, viral RNA, and inducible infectious virus by cell type.
5. Compare blood reservoir against lymph-node/tissue reservoir.

Who: Research lab only.

Sample: Blood/PBMCs; tissue only if clinically justified or research-approved.

Meaning:

- Reservoir concentrated in specific cells: delivery target clarified.
- Distributed reservoir: cure requires broader delivery.

Limits: Blood may not represent tissue.

Risks: Research sampling, anesthesia/tissue biopsy risk if applicable.

Next action: Build delivery and editing strategy around dominant reservoir cells.

Status: Research track.

## 3.2 Intact/replication-competent virus assay

Purpose: Distinguish leftover viral fragments from dangerous rebound-capable virus.

Why: PCR positivity alone may overcount dead fragments; PCR negativity may miss rare reservoirs.

Literal completion:

1. Develop or access FIV viral outgrowth assay.
2. Activate cells ex vivo.
3. Detect whether infectious FIV can be recovered.
4. Pair with cell-associated DNA/RNA measurements.
5. Repeat after any candidate cure intervention.

Who: Specialist research lab.

Sample: PBMCs or tissue cells.

Meaning:

- Infectious virus recoverable: not cured.
- No recoverable virus: promising, but must test sensitivity and rebound.

Limits: Assay sensitivity may miss rare reservoirs.

Risks: Lab biosafety; sample burden.

Next action: Use as cure-proof metric.

Status: Needed for true cure validation.

# Track 4: Proviral erasure / disabling

## 4.1 Conserved target discovery

Purpose: Find FIV genetic regions stable enough to target.

Why: Single-target editing can fail if virus mutates or varies by subtype.

Literal completion:

1. Collect FIV sequences across subtypes and from local infected cats if possible.
2. Align LTR, gag, pol, env, vif, rev, and regulatory regions.
3. Mark conserved regions essential for replication.
4. Exclude regions similar to feline genome to reduce off-target risk.
5. Design multiplex guide candidates.

Who: Computational biologist/research lab.

Data: FIV sequences; feline genome reference.

Meaning:

- Conserved essential sites found: editing target pool.
- No single conserved site enough: multiplex required.

Limits: Computational promise does not prove biological safety.

Risks: Bad guide design can produce escape or off-target injury.

Next action: In vitro guide testing.

Status: Research method logged.

## 4.2 CRISPR/base/prime-editing in infected feline cells

Purpose: Test whether provirus can be disabled without killing host cells.

Why: This is the direct “erase/disarm” cure route.

Literal completion:

1. Culture infected feline cells under approved lab conditions.
2. Deliver editing machinery transiently if possible.
3. Test single guides and multiplex guide sets.
4. Measure proviral DNA disruption, viral RNA, viral protein, infectious virus, cell viability, and off-target edits.
5. Stimulate cells to test rebound.
6. Sequence escape variants.

Who: Retrovirology/gene-editing lab.

Sample/data: Infected feline cells, FIV sequences, editing reagents.

Meaning:

- Viral output drops but cells live: candidate works in vitro.
- Infectious virus gone after stimulation: stronger candidate.
- Escape variants appear: redesign multiplex.

Limits: Cell culture success does not equal animal cure.

Risks: Off-target edits; incomplete editing; selection of resistant virus.

Next action: Delivery optimization and animal-model ethics review.

Status: Cure-discovery core method.

## 4.3 Delivery platform selection

Purpose: Get cure machinery into reservoir cells.

Why: Delivery is likely the main wall.

Literal completion:

1. List target cells from reservoir map.
2. Compare platforms: lipid nanoparticles, viral vectors, extracellular vesicles, nanoblades, ex vivo edited cells, antibody-targeted particles.
3. Score each by feline-cell tropism, transient expression, immune toxicity, tissue reach, manufacturing feasibility, and repeat-dosing possibility.
4. Test delivery in uninfected feline cells first.
5. Test in infected cells after safety screen.

Who: Gene-therapy research team.

Data: Delivery efficiency, toxicity, tissue targeting.

Meaning:

- Efficient, low-toxicity delivery: candidate platform.
- Poor delivery: cure tool cannot reach target.

Limits: In vitro delivery does not prove whole-body delivery.

Risks: Immune reaction, insertional risk for some vectors, off-target tissue exposure.

Next action: Animal-model design only after strong in vitro safety.

Status: Cure-discovery bottleneck.

# Track 5: Latency lock

## 5.1 FIV LTR repression map

Purpose: Identify how to silence provirus permanently.

Why: A functional cure may not require removing provirus if it can never reactivate.

Literal completion:

1. Map FIV LTR promoter/enhancer elements.
2. Identify host transcription factors that activate FIV.
3. Test dCas9-repressor or epigenetic repressor tools targeted to LTR.
4. Stimulate cells with inflammatory signals.
5. Measure whether virus stays silent.

Who: Molecular virology lab.

Data: FIV promoter activity, RNA/protein output, cell gene-expression effects.

Meaning:

- Durable silence after stimulation: block-and-lock candidate.
- Rebound after stress: insufficient.

Limits: Silent provirus can still be genetically present.

Risks: Broad host gene suppression; immune impairment.

Next action: Test durability and specificity.

Status: Research track.

# Track 6: Expose-and-clear

## 6.1 Latency reversal plus clearance assay

Purpose: Reveal hidden infected cells and kill them.

Why: Latent cells evade immune clearance by staying quiet.

Literal completion:

1. Identify feline-safe latency-reversing candidates in vitro.
2. Reactivate infected cells under controlled conditions.
3. Detect surface markers or viral proteins on reactivated cells.
4. Add candidate clearance tools: antibodies, cytotoxic T cells, CAR-like cells, NK-like effectors, or bispecific binders.
5. Measure killing of infected cells and sparing of uninfected cells.
6. Test inflammatory cytokine risk.

Who: Immunology/retrovirology lab.

Sample/data: Infected cells; immune effector cells; viral markers.

Meaning:

- Reactivated infected cells are selectively killed: candidate path.
- Reactivation without killing: dangerous/incomplete.

Limits: Ex vivo killing does not prove whole-cat safety.

Risks: Inflammation, cytokine storm, immune exhaustion, incomplete clearance.

Next action: Pair with antiviral blockade and reservoir assay.

Status: Research track.

# Track 7: Entry-proof immune replacement

## 7.1 CD134/CXCR4 separation-of-function map

Purpose: Determine whether FIV entry can be blocked without harming normal immunity.

Why: FIV uses feline CD134 and CXCR4-related entry steps, but these receptors have real immune functions.

Literal completion:

1. Map FIV-contact residues on feline CD134/OX40 and CXCR4.
2. Compare with residues needed for normal ligand binding and immune signaling.
3. Search for naturally resistant receptor variants in domestic cats or wild felids.
4. Engineer candidate receptor edits in feline cells.
5. Test normal immune function and FIV resistance.

Who: Structural biology / immunology / feline genetics research team.

Data: Receptor sequences, structure models, infection assays, immune signaling assays.

Meaning:

- FIV blocked while normal signaling preserved: high-value cure lead.
- Immune signaling damaged: not acceptable.

Limits: Cell resistance does not equal whole immune reconstitution.

Risks: Immune dysfunction, stem-cell trafficking problems, viral adaptation.

Next action: Restriction-factor or ex vivo cell-editing research.

Status: Research track.

## 7.2 Restriction-factor enhancement

Purpose: Add or amplify intracellular blocks against FIV replication.

Why: If entry cannot be safely blocked, intracellular restriction may stop replication after entry.

Literal completion:

1. Identify feline or cross-species restriction factors affecting FIV.
2. Test APOBEC/TRIM/tetherin/SAMHD1-like pathways and FIV evasion systems such as Vif.
3. Engineer resistant versions that FIV cannot easily counter.
4. Test replication, cell viability, and immune function.

Who: Retroviral host-factor lab.

Data: Gene-expression, viral replication, cell health.

Meaning:

- Strong FIV block with preserved cell function: candidate armoring method.

Limits: May suppress replication without eradicating existing reservoirs.

Risks: Immune dysregulation; viral escape.

Next action: Combine with reservoir reduction.

Status: Research track.

# Track 8: Immune ecological conversion

## 8.1 Nonprogressor comparison

Purpose: Find why some FIV+ cats remain stable.

Why: A functional cure may come from turning progressors into controllers.

Literal completion:

1. Identify long-term FIV+ nonprogressor cats.
2. Compare against progressor cats by viral load, immune activation, cytokines, oral/gut inflammation, microbiome, lymphocyte subsets, and genetics.
3. Identify protective patterns.
4. Test whether interventions can reproduce those patterns.

Who: Veterinary school / research cohort.

Data: Blood, oral exam, fecal/microbiome, immune profiling, clinical history.

Meaning:

- Protective signature found: functional-cure target.

Limits: Correlation is not causation.

Risks: Overinterpreting small cohorts.

Next action: Test causal interventions.

Status: Research track.

# Track 9: Prevention in FIV-negative cats

## 9.1 Baseline FIV/FeLV testing before mixing cats

Purpose: Prevent unknown transmission risk.

Why: The most practical prevention is not letting untested exposure patterns happen blindly.

Literal completion:

1. Test every cat’s FIV/FeLV status before introduction or after unknown-risk exposure.
2. Record test brand/type/date/result.
3. Retest after the window period if recent exposure is possible.
4. Do not rely on appearance; healthy-looking cats can test positive.
5. Keep records because vaccine history can affect interpretation.

Who: Vet/lab.

Sample: Blood.

Meaning:

- Negative: lower risk, but recent exposure may need retest.
- Positive: separate housing/transmission discussion.
- Discordant: confirmatory testing.

Limits: One negative test after recent exposure may be premature.

Risks: False reassurance if timing is wrong.

Next action: Retest if exposure window; household plan.

Status: Recommended for prevention.

## 9.2 Bite-prevention household design

Purpose: Prevent the main transmission route.

Why: FIV spreads primarily through deep bite wounds. Casual stable cohabitation is much lower risk than fighting.

Literal completion:

1. Identify cats who fight, chase, corner, mount, stalk, or guard resources.
2. Separate feeding stations.
3. Add multiple litter boxes, water stations, vertical spaces, hiding spaces, and escape paths.
4. Do gradual introductions through scent, sight, barrier, and supervised contact.
5. Never allow repeated fights to “work themselves out.”
6. Separate during high-stress events: moving, vet return, illness, new animals, food conflict.

Who: Owner; vet behaviorist if severe.

Data: Fight log, wounds, behavior observations.

Meaning:

- No fights/no bite wounds: major prevention success.
- Any deep bite: exposure event.

Limits: Does not prevent all theoretical transmission.

Risks: Underestimating subtle aggression.

Next action: Post-bite vet care and testing schedule.

Status: Recommended for all FIV-negative cats.

## 9.3 Indoor-only / no roaming exposure

Purpose: Prevent bite exposure from unknown cats.

Why: Outdoor fights with untested cats are a major FIV risk.

Literal completion:

1. Keep FIV-negative cats indoors or in secure catio/harness-supervised settings.
2. Prevent intact roaming behavior with spay/neuter.
3. Do not allow unsupervised contact with unknown outdoor cats.
4. Treat wounds immediately.

Who: Owner/vet.

Data: Exposure history, wound history.

Meaning: Reduces primary transmission pathway.

Limits: Indoor cats still need testing if exposed before adoption.

Risks: Indoor stress if enrichment is poor; solve with environment.

Next action: Enrichment and behavior plan.

Status: Recommended prevention foundation.

## 9.4 FIV vaccine decision map for FIV-negative cats

Purpose: Log the vaccine method honestly: what it tried to do, why it is limited, and how future vaccines could improve.

Why: A killed dual-subtype FIV vaccine existed, but FIV vaccine protection has been complicated by viral diversity, incomplete protection across strains, and interference with FIV antibody-test interpretation. In many places it is unavailable/discontinued, so prevention cannot depend on it.

Literal completion for current practical decision:

1. Ask vet whether any FIV vaccine is currently licensed/available in the country/state.
2. If available, test cat FIV-negative before any vaccination.
3. Record microchip, vaccine brand, lot, date, and site.
4. Discuss lifestyle risk: outdoor fighting risk, intact status, high-conflict household, exposure to FIV-positive cats.
5. Discuss that vaccination may complicate later antibody-based FIV test interpretation.
6. Decide only after risk/benefit review with vet.
7. Keep permanent vaccine documentation.

Who: Veterinarian.

Sample/data: Pre-vaccine FIV test; vaccine record.

Meaning:

- Vaccination, if available, may reduce risk against some strains but is not universal protection.
- No vaccine does not mean no prevention; bite prevention remains central.

Limits:

- Does not guarantee protection.
- May affect antibody-test interpretation.
- May not be available.

Risks:

- Vaccine adverse reactions.
- False confidence leading to bite exposure.
- Diagnostic confusion later.

Next action:

- If unavailable: use non-vaccine prevention tracks.
- If available and cat is high-risk: vet-guided decision.
- If low-risk indoor stable cat: vaccine may not be justified.

Status: Prevention track, vet-dependent.

## 9.5 Future vaccine discovery method

Purpose: Map how prevention could be improved beyond current/old vaccine limits.

Why: A useful future vaccine must handle viral diversity and avoid diagnostic confusion.

Literal completion:

1. Build sequence database of FIV env/gag/pol across subtypes and regions.
2. Identify conserved neutralizing epitopes and T-cell epitopes.
3. Compare natural nonprogressors and exposed-uninfected cats for protective immune signatures.
4. Design multivalent vaccine candidates covering more subtype diversity.
5. Test whether vaccine creates neutralizing antibodies, cell-mediated immunity, and protection against heterologous challenge.
6. Design diagnostic differentiation: DIVA strategy, marker vaccine, or tests that distinguish vaccine antibody from infection.
7. Validate protection without causing antibody-dependent enhancement or immune activation that increases target cells.

Who: Veterinary vaccine research team.

Data: Viral sequences, immune assays, challenge studies, diagnostic assay design.

Meaning:

- Broad neutralization plus T-cell protection: stronger vaccine candidate.
- DIVA-compatible design: solves testing confusion.

Limits: Animal challenge ethics and strain diversity remain hard.

Risks: Incomplete protection, diagnostic confusion, immune activation.

Next action: Preclinical vaccine pipeline.

Status: Research prevention track.

# Track 10: Evidence standard before claiming cure

## 10.1 Minimum cure evidence

A claim of FIV cure requires more than “looks better.” It requires:

1. Clinical improvement.
2. Viral RNA suppression.
3. Cell-associated DNA/provirus assessment.
4. Replication-competent virus assay or viral outgrowth testing if available.
5. No rebound after immune activation or time off intervention.
6. Reservoir testing in relevant cells/tissues.
7. Safety profile in feline genome and immune function.
8. Long-term follow-up.

## 10.2 What does not prove cure

- Appetite improved.
- Lymph nodes shrank.
- A PCR was negative once.
- Antibody test changed.
- Viral load dropped temporarily.
- A supplement helped energy.
- A cat lived for years while FIV-positive.
- A cell-culture result worked once.

All of those can matter. None alone proves cure.

## 10.3 Completion definition

The cure-discovery project is not complete until at least one candidate path shows:

- Targeted mechanism.
- Delivery to reservoir cells.
- Replication-competent virus eliminated or permanently silenced.
- No rebound.
- Cat survives with functional immune system.
- Prevention logic exists for uninfected cats.

# Immediate O'Malley action list

1. Resting respiratory rate log.
2. Vet lymph-node map.
3. FNA/cytology of enlarged node(s).
4. CBC with differential.
5. Chemistry panel + urinalysis.
6. Thoracic imaging if breathing is affected.
7. Confirm FeLV status if not already documented.
8. Preserve every test result as structured data in the repo.

# Immediate prevention action list for FIV-negative cats

1. Confirm FIV/FeLV status of each cat.
2. Prevent deep bites: no uncontrolled fighting, no outdoor roaming.
3. Separate high-conflict animals before injury occurs.
4. Use vet-guided vaccine discussion only if vaccine is available and exposure risk is high.
5. Keep permanent records of testing and any FIV vaccination because it affects interpretation.
