# Animal Recovery v6 Medical Source Anchors

## Voice-readable summary

This file records public veterinary source anchors for the Animal Recovery Scientific Health Upgrade lane. These anchors support question-building and escalation logic, but they do not replace diagnosis by a veterinarian.

## Source boundary

Use these sources to structure veterinary questions and documentation.

Do not use them to self-diagnose, self-prescribe, or bypass veterinary care.

## Lymph nodes / lymphoma / FeLV-FIV direction

### Merck Veterinary Manual: lymphoma and lymph-node cytology

Relevant anchor:

- Generalized, nonpainful lymphadenopathy can raise suspicion for lymphoma in dogs.
- Fine-needle aspiration with cytologic evaluation and tissue biopsy with histologic evaluation are diagnostic methods for lymphoma.
- Merck's cytology guidance notes that lymph-node aspirates can help distinguish hyperplasia from neoplasia, but biopsy may be required if there is doubt or if therapy decisions depend on confirmation.

Why this matters for O'Malley:

O'Malley's enlarged lymph nodes should be converted into evidence: FNA/cytology if the vet agrees, with biopsy/escalation if inconclusive or if treatment decisions require stronger confirmation.

Public anchors:

- Merck Veterinary Manual, Lymphoma in Dogs: https://www.merckvetmanual.com/circulatory-system/canine-lymphoma/canine-lymphoma
- Merck Veterinary Manual, Cytology / lymph nodes: https://www.merckvetmanual.com/clinical-pathology-and-procedures/diagnostic-procedures-for-the-private-practice-laboratory/cytology

### Merck Veterinary Manual: FeLV

Relevant anchor:

- FeLV is an important infectious disease of cats.
- Affected cats can develop anemia, cancers, and immune suppression.

Why this matters for O'Malley:

FeLV/FIV status matters because viral/immune disease can alter cancer risk, immune vulnerability, infection risk, and treatment planning.

Public anchor:

- Merck Veterinary Manual, Feline Leukemia Virus: https://www.merckvetmanual.com/cat-owners/disorders-affecting-multiple-body-systems-of-cats/feline-leukemia-virus-felv

## Glaucoma / hypertension / systemic disease direction

### Cornell Feline Health Center: feline glaucoma

Relevant anchor:

- Glaucoma involves fluid drainage problems that increase pressure in the eye.
- Pressure damages the optic nerve and can lead to partial or total blindness.
- Diagnosis can be confirmed using a tonometer to measure intraocular pressure.
- Referral to a veterinary ophthalmologist may be advised for suspected, diagnosed, or complicated glaucoma.

Why this matters for Bug/Bugsy:

Eye cloudiness, eye pain, squinting, or glaucoma history should not be treated as cosmetic. Eye pressure measurement and vision-protection planning are high-value.

Public anchor:

- Cornell Feline Health Center, Feline Glaucoma: https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/feline-glaucoma

### Cornell Feline Health Center: hypertension

Relevant anchor:

- Secondary hypertension is common in cats and often related to systemic disease.
- Chronic kidney disease and hyperthyroidism are common related diseases.
- Blood pressure is measured with Doppler or oscillometric cuff methods.
- Target organ injury can affect the eyes, brain, kidney, and heart.
- High blood pressure can cause ocular injury, neurologic signs, kidney effects, and cardiac changes including murmurs or abnormal heart sounds.

Why this matters for Bug/Bugsy:

A heart murmur plus eye-pressure concerns creates a reasonable vet-question path: blood pressure, eye pressure, kidney values, thyroid/metabolic screening if indicated, and heart follow-up.

Public anchor:

- Cornell Feline Health Center, Hypertension: https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/cornell-feline-health-center/health-information/feline-health-topics/hypertension

## Skin / scabs / shared-environment direction

### AAHA: feline skin disease diagnostics

Relevant anchor:

- Infectious and parasitic causes should generally be ruled in or out before investigating underlying allergy or other causes.
- Core feline dermatologic diagnostics can include flea combing, superficial/deep skin scrapings, impression cytology or acetate tape preparations, Wood's lamp evaluation, dermatophyte culture/PCR testing, and antimite/antiparasitic treatment trials.
- Negative skin scraping does not always rule out some mite species.

Why this matters for Griffey and Nimby:

Scabs in multiple animals should be treated as a shared-environment and possible-contagion question until parasites, dermatophytes/ringworm, infection, and allergy are sorted.

Public anchors:

- AAHA, Skin Diseases in Cats: https://www.aaha.org/trends-magazine/august-2023/gs-skin-diseases-in-cats/
- AAHA, Diagnosing Allergic Skin Disease in the Feline Patient: https://www.aaha.org/resources/2023-aaha-management-of-allergic-skin-diseases-in-dogs-and-cats-guidelines/figure-6-diagnosing-allergic-skin-disease-in-the-feline-patient/

## Emergency boundary

Emergency signs are outside the normal health-upgrade loop. They override tracking and require urgent/emergency veterinary contact.

Examples include major breathing changes, collapse, severe weakness, seizures, toxin exposure, eye injury/pain, abnormal gums, urinary obstruction, repeated vomiting/diarrhea, or rapid decline. Exact urgency should be determined by a veterinarian or emergency veterinary service.
