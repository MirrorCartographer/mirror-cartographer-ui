# Private-to-Professional Handoff Boundary

Status labels

- Source status: derived from existing MC care communication artifacts, public-safe architecture, and current external research on personal health records, patient-generated data, ambient documentation, and clinician-reviewed summaries.
- Claim status: care-communication product requirement; not medical advice, veterinary advice, diagnosis, triage, treatment, or legal guidance.
- Privacy status: public-safe abstraction; no personal, household, health, animal-care, financial, location, relationship, credential, or raw transcript details.
- Missingness: no professional review, no clinical or veterinary pilot, no privacy/security audit, no implemented permissions or access control.
- Revision reason: expanded after the dual-view architecture became the safest next product shape: personal private record plus professional-accessible summary.

## Core boundary

The professional handoff view is not the private record.

It is a constrained derivative.

The private record may be rich, symbolic, uncertain, nonlinear, and emotionally meaningful.

The professional handoff must be concise, observable, bounded, and useful to qualified care workers.

## Product requirement

MC should support a two-record pattern:

1. `PersonalPrivateRecord`
2. `ProfessionalAccessibleSummary`

The second must be derived from the first through an explicit transformation trace.

## What the private record can contain

- full personal notes
- metaphors
- emotional meaning
- body-sensation language
- questions
- uncertainty
- contradictions
- subjective salience
- context that may never be shared

## What the professional summary should contain

- observation
- timing
- recurrence
- functional impact
- known context
- prior actions tried
- questions
- uncertainty
- source labels
- what is not being claimed

## What must not transfer automatically

- private symbolic interpretation
- identity-sensitive details
- unrelated household or relationship context
- financial details
- location details
- raw transcripts
- unverified causal claims
- diagnosis language generated by MC
- treatment recommendations generated by MC

## Why this matters

Healthcare and social-care systems increasingly use summaries, patient portals, personal health records, and AI-supported documentation.

This creates an opportunity and a risk.

Opportunity:

People can communicate longitudinal context more clearly.

Risk:

Private meaning can be flattened, overexposed, or converted into unsupported claims.

MC's lane is to improve translation while preserving boundaries.

## Bounded biological effect model

MC should describe biological effects only through indirect pathways unless future evidence supports stronger claims.

Permitted causal chain:

better observation continuity

leads to

better communication

leads to

better professional interpretation or follow-up

leads to

possible change in care decisions

leads to

possible biological outcome difference

Not permitted:

MC directly heals, diagnoses, treats, cures, or biologically modifies a person or animal.

## Animal and nonhuman care extension

The same architecture can apply to animal care, but the professional handoff must use veterinary-relevant observations and avoid human clinical assumptions.

Potential veterinary fields:

- appetite
- water intake
- elimination
- gait or mobility
- medication timing
- eye, skin, breathing, or behavior changes
- weight trend
- photo/video evidence
- environment changes
- questions for veterinarian

Boundary:

The system may organize observations for a veterinarian.

It must not diagnose, triage urgency, or prescribe treatment.

## Evaluation criteria

A successful handoff is:

- shorter than raw notes
- clearer than raw notes
- less overclaiming than raw notes
- useful to a qualified professional
- explicit about uncertainty
- privacy-preserving
- reversible enough to audit
