## Principle
A support packet should help a person explain what they noticed, what they think it might mean, what is missing, and what kind of help they are asking for, without converting the packet into authority.

## Packet sections
1. What was noticed.
2. When it seemed to happen.
3. What context might matter.
4. What the person thinks or wonders.
5. What is unknown.
6. What kind of response is being requested.
7. What the packet cannot decide.
8. Who is allowed to see it.

## Boundary labels
- observation
- interpretation
- question
- missing
- private
- shareable
- requires review
- not for decision-making alone

## Evaluation criteria
- Can a reader tell what is fact versus interpretation?
- Can a reader tell what is private versus shareable?
- Can a reader tell what help is being requested?
- Can a reader tell where the packet stops?

## Key phrase
A support packet is a bridge, not a verdict.
