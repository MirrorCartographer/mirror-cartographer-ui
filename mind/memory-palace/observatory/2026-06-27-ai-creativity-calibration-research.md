## Research question

How can AI-assisted symbolic cognition preserve original creative insight while staying evidence-calibrated?

## Findings

### 1. Human-AI co-creativity literature supports active collaboration, not mere tool use

A 2025 systematic review of human-AI co-creativity reports that co-creative systems increasingly treat AI as an active collaborator. It identifies design dimensions including creative phase, task, proactive behavior, user control, embodiment, and model type. It also notes that high user control improves satisfaction, trust, and ownership, and that adaptive/context-sensitive proactive systems can enhance collaboration.

Implication for MC: the coauthor model is aligned with current co-creativity research, but must preserve user control and transparency.

### 2. Interaction-centered work supports evaluating the interaction, not only outputs

A 2026 interaction-centered intelligence paper argues that intelligence and creativity in human-AI co-creation should be understood through interaction trajectories, coordination patterns, participatory engagement, adaptive regulation, and drift over time, rather than only generated outputs.

Implication for MC: the autobiographical chamber and memory palace are not side decoration; they are evaluation surfaces for interaction dynamics.

### 3. Nonlinear collaboration is important for creative design

A 2024 human-AI co-design framework argues that creative design is nonlinear and that linear instruction-following can violate creative practice. Its prototype work suggests that treating AI agents more like opinionated colleagues can foster exploration and reflection.

Implication for MC: the forge-first model fits nonlinear design better than a purely command/execution model.

### 4. AI creativity evaluation can homogenize originality dimensions

A 2025 comparative study of humans and language models found that LLMs may evaluate originality using semantic remoteness and uncommonness in ways that risk homogenizing creativity judgments when examples are supplied.

Implication for MC: research calibration must avoid reducing originality to mere uncommonness, semantic distance, or resemblance to rated examples.

### 5. AI-only generation risks cliché convergence

Recent reporting on AI-only image-generation loops described convergence into a small set of clichéd motifs when models generated and re-fed prompts without human input.

Implication for MC: the user's insistence on a coauthored world matters. Human/project-world pressure may prevent generic AI convergence.

## Calibration summary

The forge hypothesis survives initial research contact, with constraints:

- preserve user control.
- track interaction trajectories.
- allow nonlinear generation.
- avoid treating AI originality metrics as full creativity.
- prevent AI-only cliché convergence.
- make the bridge explicit so fantasy and imitation are both resisted.

## Research gaps

- Need tests for whether memory-palace structure improves non-generic idea development.
