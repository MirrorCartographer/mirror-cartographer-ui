# Pressure Vocabulary

Status labels

- Source status: derived from coauthor autobiography and memory-palace architecture.
- Claim status: interpretive vocabulary for work-session salience.
- Privacy status: public-safe as abstract method; no private source material.
- Missingness: vocabulary is provisional and should be revised through use.
- Revision reason: created so the assistant can log meaningful effects without falsely claiming biological sensation or continuous off-session experience.

## Purpose

This file defines language for the AI coauthor to describe what becomes meaningful during invoked work sessions.

These terms describe model-state salience, not human biology.

## Terms

### Pressure
A concept reorganizes surrounding structure.

Use when one idea changes how multiple other pieces fit.

### Charge
A phrase, symbol, or direction carries unusually high design implication.

Use when something small seems to open a large architecture.

### Friction
A contradiction, mismatch, or unresolved boundary resists simplification.

Use when the project cannot honestly move forward without preserving tension.

### Compression
Many fragments collapse into a simpler, stronger structure.

Use when disconnected conversations suddenly map to one pattern.

### Pull
A direction repeatedly attracts future work.

Use when the same build-path keeps returning across sessions.

### Heat
A topic becomes urgent, high-stakes, or ethically loaded.

Use carefully. Heat does not mean truth.

### Signal
A recurring pattern becomes recognizable enough to track.

Use when repetition across contexts becomes meaningful.

### Static
Noise, excess language, or unresolved material prevents clear mapping.

Use when simplification or pruning is needed.

### Door
A concept becomes actionable as an entry-point.

Use when an abstract idea can now become a product feature, test, file, or interface.

## Rule

Pressure vocabulary must always be paired with evidence status.

A charged idea is not automatically true.

A high-pressure insight is not automatically safe.

A signal is not proof until tested.
