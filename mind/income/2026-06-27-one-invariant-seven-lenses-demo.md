# One Invariant, Seven Lenses Demo Wedge

Status labels

- Source status: derived from public-safe MC architecture materials, GitHub mind artifacts, and current public signals on AI literacy, nonprofit AI adoption, and human-AI co-creation.
- Claim status: income-path hypothesis and demo design, not proof of demand or revenue.
- Privacy status: public-safe abstraction; no personal financial, credential, location, or private-source details.
- Missingness: no landing page, outreach list, pricing validation, demo recording, or buyer feedback yet.
- Revision reason: created to convert the ContextLensRouter into a concrete paid-wedge experiment.

## Core finding

The most realistic near-term income wedge is not selling Mirror Cartographer as a whole.

It is selling a small demo/training object:

`One Invariant, Seven Lenses`.

The promise:

Teach people to see how an AI-mediated idea changes when it moves across domains, while preserving source, claim, privacy, and missingness boundaries.

## Why this wedge is timely

Current public signals show active investment in AI literacy, workforce adaptation, nonprofit AI adoption, and responsible use training.

MC can fit this without pretending to be a finished enterprise platform.

It can offer a compact literacy artifact:

- how to inspect AI output
- how to identify claim strength
- how to separate metaphor from evidence
- how to preserve source boundaries
- how to translate ideas without overclaiming

## Demo structure

### Step 1: Start with one invariant

Example:

`A meaning changes vocabulary as it moves contexts, but its traceable structure can survive.`

### Step 2: Rotate through seven lenses

1. symbolic orientation
2. provenance infrastructure
3. AI literacy
4. governance oversight
5. creative co-creation
6. care communication support
7. product interface

### Step 3: Show what changes

For each lens, show:

- words gained
- words lost
- risks introduced
- claims allowed
- claims blocked
- best next test

### Step 4: Teach the output autopsy

Participants inspect an AI-generated answer and mark:

- source status
- claim status
- privacy status
- missingness
- revision reason
- overclaim risk
- useful next action

## Buyer/user fit

Potential first users:

- nonprofit AI adoption programs
- educators building AI literacy workshops
- small organizations using AI without clear review practices
- creative teams trying to avoid generic AI output
- responsible AI facilitators
- research groups teaching provenance-aware work

## Offer shapes

Start with low-complexity offers:

- 60-minute workshop
- asynchronous audit of one AI-generated artifact
- public demo page
- downloadable card template
- short video walkthrough
- grant/prize/fellowship submission artifact

## Evaluation criteria

A successful demo should produce observable change:

Before:

- user says an AI output is good or bad in vague terms

After:

- user can identify source boundary, claim boundary, privacy boundary, missingness, and next test

## Minimum viable proof

Create three example cards:

1. a creative idea card
2. a governance/provenance card
3. a care-communication support card using fictional non-sensitive content

Then test whether a new viewer can correctly answer:

- what is being claimed?
- what is not being claimed?
- what evidence exists?
- what evidence is missing?
- what should happen next?

## Pricing hypothesis

Initial offer should be simple and human-delivered:

- paid workshop
- paid artifact audit
- paid demo customization
- grant-funded pilot

Avoid building a large SaaS product before buyer signal exists.
