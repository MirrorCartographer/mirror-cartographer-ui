## Core finding

The most legible paid wedge is not `symbolic AI` by itself.

It is:

`Make AI outputs inspectable, teachable, and beautiful enough that people actually want to understand them.`

## Offer concept

`Output Autopsy: Beautiful AI Literacy for Real Teams`

A compact workshop or demo package that turns one AI-generated artifact into a visually inspectable card.

The card shows:

- what sources were used
- what claims are allowed
- what claims are blocked
- what is private or public-safe
- what is missing
- what changed through revision
- what the next test should be

## Why this is practical

Current public signals suggest rising demand for AI literacy, workforce transition, responsible adoption, and transparent AI workflows.

The gap is that most AI literacy materials are either too generic or too technical.

MC can occupy a middle lane:

- practical enough for teams
- visual enough for nontechnical users
- bounded enough for responsible use
- unusual enough to be memorable

## Possible buyers/users

- nonprofits adopting AI
- educators teaching AI literacy
- small organizations creating AI policies
- creative teams using generative tools
- product teams evaluating AI outputs
- responsible AI workshops
- workforce-transition programs

## Minimal demo

Take a single AI output and produce three versions:

1. Raw output
2. Ordinary critique
3. BeautyCompressionCard

The demo succeeds only if a new viewer can answer:

- What can this output safely claim?
- What should not be trusted yet?
- What source boundary matters?
- What action should happen next?

## Pricing hypothesis

Start with services, not software subscriptions.

Possible first offers:

- paid mini-workshop
- one-off output audit
- custom provenance card for a team artifact
- AI literacy training module
- nonprofit responsible-AI adoption package
- creative workflow demo

## Evidence needed

Before treating this as a real business path, test:

- whether nontechnical users understand the card faster than a written critique
- whether teams find the visual boundary system useful
- whether buyers prefer AI literacy, governance, or creative-workflow framing
- whether the artifact creates enough desire to justify payment
