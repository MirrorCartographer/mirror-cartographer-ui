## Core finding

The strongest near-term paid wedge is a demo that helps people understand how one messy input becomes multiple safe outputs.

Working name:

`Private Notes to Professional Summary: Output Autopsy`

## Buyer problem

Teams are adopting AI faster than they understand its transformations.

They need to know:

- what was preserved
- what was compressed
- what was removed
- what was overclaimed
- what source boundaries remain
- what should not be shared
- how to audit a generated summary

## Demo promise

Show one fictional messy note routed into five views:

1. private reflection
2. professional handoff
3. care-team task view
4. public-safe method view
5. research-safe aggregate view

Then show the transformation trace between them.

## Why this can generate income

This is not selling MC as a giant unfinished universe.

It sells one understandable skill:

`AI output provenance literacy for real-world summaries.`

Potential buyers or users:

- small organizations using AI summaries
- educators teaching AI literacy
- care-navigation groups
- nonprofits using intake forms
- consultants who need safer client summaries
- creators who need public/private boundary tools

## Format options

### Small paid workshop

60 to 90 minutes.

Deliverable:

- one fictional demo
- one output autopsy worksheet
- one source-boundary checklist

### Self-serve template

A downloadable Notion, PDF, or web template.

Deliverable:

- input-to-output trace sheet
- claim boundary checklist
- privacy boundary checklist

### Audit service

Review a team's AI-generated summaries and identify:

- unsupported claims
- missing source labels
- privacy leakage
- overcompression
- unclear audience routing

## Minimum viable proof

Build one polished fictional example.

The example must be beautiful enough to understand quickly and rigorous enough to show real judgment.

## Evaluation criteria

The demo is viable if a stranger can answer after five minutes:

- what changed between views
- why it changed
- what was protected
- what would be unsafe to publish
- what a professional could use
- what remains unknown
