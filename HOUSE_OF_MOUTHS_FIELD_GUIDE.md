# The House of Mouths
*A field guide to the companion world*

> A word is not a label placed on a thing. It is a small room where a thing can change shape.

This repository is the **encounter side** of Mirror Cartographer: interactive experiments, language, atmosphere, prototypes, and small worlds that let an idea be felt before it is explained.

The Observatory asks, *What can we responsibly know?*  
The House asks, *What happens when we enter the question from another angle?*

Neither question cancels the other.

## The syntax field

A name can be read as a sound, a shape, a sequence, a room, a rhythm, or a question. None of those readings has to become the only reading.

- **C → Ca → Cas** — the first letter gathers a vowel, then begins to become a name.
- **Ch** — a hinge: two letters, one breath, a different entrance.
- **CAS** — a compressed signature; a constellation without its connecting lines.
- **SAC** — the same pieces turned around; order changes what the eye expects.
- **Cha** — a small opening sound, almost a gesture.
- **Chair** — a place to sit inside a word; an object hidden in a sequence of letters.
- **Ity** — a suffix that makes a quality feel like a state of being.
- **Aless** — a name-fragment with room left at the edge.

These are playful readings, not etymological claims. Let a word hold several possibilities without pretending they all have the same evidence.

## A pocket lexicon

**Nullglow** — the outline left by an absence that matters.  
**Glyphfracture** — when a symbol splits into readings that cannot be reduced to one.  
**Driftroot** — a pattern that keeps returning before its origin is understood.  
**Tonefold** — the change in meaning caused by a shift in tone or phrasing.  
**Lurecraft** — an invitation that creates curiosity without dictating the answer.  
**Mirrorgrain** — the direction in which a reflection distorts what it reflects.  
**Soft-contradiction** — two statements that seem incompatible until their hidden conditions are named.  
**Aftermeaning** — the meaning that arrives only after an experience has passed.

Treat the words as instruments for attention. Keep the ones that open a door; revise or discard the ones that become cages.

## Questions that have more than one entrance

- What part of me is a fact, and what part is the story I learned to tell around it?
- If two opposite descriptions both feel true, what variable have I left out?
- What would this idea look like if it were a room, a weather system, a body, or a song?
- Which detail is carrying the weight of the whole interpretation?
- What becomes visible when I reverse the order?
- What am I calling a flaw because I have not found its function yet?
- If the map is accurate but lifeless, what kind of information has it failed to preserve?
- What if the contradiction is not an error, but a direction marker?

A good question does not demand confession. It offers a handle.

## The rules of this house

1. **No single lens owns the whole person.**
2. **Symbolic meaning can be emotionally real without being empirical evidence.**
3. **A metaphor may open a hypothesis; it cannot certify one.**
4. **Beauty is not decoration pasted over function. It can be a way to notice.**
5. **A broken interaction is a real failure, not a poetic success.**
6. **Keep the language alive, but keep the routes, controls, and claims understandable.**
7. **Never make private pain the price of entry.**
