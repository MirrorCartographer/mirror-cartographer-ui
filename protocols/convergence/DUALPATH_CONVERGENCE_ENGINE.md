# Dualpath Convergence Engine

## Voice-readable summary

Dualpath convergence is the general machine behind the ARC solver, the music taste engine, safe bounty reporting, and art generation.

It does not mean the system has human preference or certainty. It means a candidate earns selection when two separated forms of reasoning independently support it.

## Core formula

Attempt A: hostile courtroom.
This path asks whether the candidate survives evidence pressure, reproducibility, proof gates, and skeptical review.

Attempt B: native pattern sense.
This path asks whether the candidate has field coherence, internal structure, symbolic density, and pattern life.

Convergence:
If both paths independently reach the same candidate, that candidate becomes more trustworthy than either path alone.

## Rules

1. Generate independently first.
2. Lock outputs before comparison.
3. Compare only after both tracks are locked.
4. Treat convergence as evidence, not omniscience.
5. Treat divergence as useful information, not failure.
6. Keep proof boundaries explicit.

## ARC version

The grid is the field. The training examples are visible evidence. The hidden rule is the structure to infer. The output is a pixel-perfect candidate grid.

Attempt A asks: what answer survives exact training fit and proof pressure?
Attempt B asks: what answer follows the strongest pattern completion?

## Music version

The artist is the field. Public evidence is one training signal. The hidden rule is the world the artist is building.

Attempt A asks: does the artist survive evidence pressure?
Attempt B asks: does the artist feel like a coherent world, not just a product?

## Safety/reporting version

The issue is the field. The scope/rules are the evaluator. The output is a safe, reproducible report.

Attempt A asks: does the report survive policy, scope, reproducibility, and impact pressure?
Attempt B asks: does the pattern reveal a real systemic failure worth explaining?

## Art version

The artwork is the field. The audience and artist are two different evaluators.

Attempt A asks: can a viewer receive the work?
Attempt B asks: does the work remain true to the internal field?

## Strongest case

The strongest case is not when one path dominates. The strongest case is when both paths independently choose the same output.

Phrase:
Evidence says it matters. Pattern says it is alive.
