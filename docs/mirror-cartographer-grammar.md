# Mirror Cartographer Grammar

Version: 0.1
Status: Foundational grammar / build contract
Purpose: preserve understanding, not merely content.

Mirror Cartographer is a grammar for mapping experience across symbolic, somatic, cognitive, practical, and evidentiary layers. Its central rule is: meaning is allowed; accuracy is required. A symbol may be useful without being proven. A feeling may be real without being literal. A body signal may be important without being an oracle. A pattern may be worth tracking without becoming a command.

This grammar exists because the project drifted from a reflection tool into a continuity system. The durable object is not a chat, a summary, or a single artifact. The durable object is the relationship graph: what connects to what, how it changes, what evidence supports it, and what action is safe.

## 1. Primitive units

Everything entered into Mirror Cartographer should be expressible through these primitives.

### 1.1 Node

A node is anything that can be named and revisited.

Examples: Bugsy, right eye pressure, money, California, boredom, OpenAI, a white spot, a song lyric, a dream image, a body sensation, a research claim, a task.

Required fields:

- Name
- Domain
- Scale
- Current status
- Evidence level
- Emotional charge
- Last updated

### 1.2 Edge

An edge is a typed relationship between nodes.

Allowed edge types:

- causes
- correlates with
- echoes
- symbolizes
- constrains
- enables
- protects
- threatens
- amplifies
- inhibits
- translates into
- depends on
- conflicts with
- repairs
- measures
- predicts
- transforms into
- belongs to
- emerges from

Every edge should be labeled. Unlabeled connections create symbolic fog.

### 1.3 Layer

A layer separates kinds of truth so they do not collapse into each other.

Core layers:

1. Observed fact: what happened, what was seen, what was measured.
2. Felt response: what the body or emotion registered.
3. Possible meaning: what it might symbolize, imply, echo, or reveal.
4. Testable action: what can be checked, done, ruled out, or safely tried.

Layer rule: never let layer 2 become layer 1, never let layer 3 become layer 4, and never let urgency erase uncertainty.

### 1.4 Transformation

A transformation describes how a node changes state over time.

Transformation fields:

- Before state
- Trigger
- Mechanism hypothesis
- After state
- Evidence
- Confidence
- Next observation

Examples:

- confusion becomes orientation
- pain becomes a symptom log
- a symbol becomes a design element
- fear becomes a veterinary question
- money pressure becomes a ranked action path

### 1.5 Constraint

A constraint is a limit that shapes possible action.

Constraint types:

- physical
- financial
- emotional
- medical
- technical
- legal
- ethical
- time-based
- attention-based

Constraint rule: constraints are not failures. They are map geometry.

### 1.6 Evidence

Evidence is the support level for a claim or connection.

Evidence levels:

- Direct record: directly stated, observed, measured, or documented.
- Repeated pattern: appears across multiple entries or contexts.
- Plausible inference: follows from structure but is not directly proven.
- Symbolic resonance: meaningful but not evidential by itself.
- Speculation: possible, not yet grounded.
- Rejected: considered and ruled out.

Evidence rule: resonance can initiate inquiry, but it cannot close inquiry.

### 1.7 Charge

Charge is the intensity carried by a node, edge, or interpretation.

Charge dimensions:

- emotional intensity
- body intensity
- urgency
- repetition
- fear/hope polarity
- symbolic glow
- action pressure

Charge rule: high charge means slow down, separate layers, and increase evidence discipline.

### 1.8 Drift

Drift is movement away from the original goal or layer boundary.

Common drift patterns:

- orientation becomes proof-demand
- proof becomes external validation
- money becomes rescue fantasy
- symbolism becomes command
- health mapping becomes diagnosis certainty
- AI support becomes savior/failure binary
- artifact creation becomes identity judgment

Drift rule: when drift is detected, return to the smallest safe action.

## 2. Domains

Every node belongs to one or more domains.

Primary domains:

- Body
- Animal care
- Money
- Home and safety
- AI and software
- Symbol and myth
- Emotion
- Relationship
- Art and music
- Research and evidence
- Public proof
- Governance and ethics

Domain rule: cross-domain mapping is allowed only when the relationship type is explicit.

Bad: eye pressure means AI alignment.
Good: eye pressure and AI alignment both involve signal interpretation under uncertainty; relationship type = structural analogy, not causal claim.

## 3. Scale

Every claim exists at a scale.

Scales:

- sensation
- gesture
- body system
- animal/person
- room
- household
- project
- institution
- culture
- ecology
- time/history

Scale rule: do not use a claim from one scale as proof at another scale without a bridge.

## 4. The core sentence form

Every useful MC statement should be convertible into this sentence:

At [scale], [node A] is related to [node B] by [edge type], with [evidence level], carrying [charge], producing or suggesting [transformation/action].

Example:

At the animal-care scale, Bugsy's eye swelling is related to travel stress by possible correlation, with direct observation but uncertain mechanism, carrying high fear charge, suggesting a vet-facing symptom timeline rather than a conclusion.

## 5. The four-question engine

Every charged pattern passes through four questions:

1. What happened?
2. What did I feel?
3. What might it mean?
4. What can I test or do safely?

This is the basic soft-machine loop.

## 6. Translation grammar

A concept can be translated across domains only by preserving structure.

Translation template:

- Source concept
- Source domain
- Structural pattern
- Target domain
- Preserved relationship
- Lost detail
- Evidence boundary

Example:

Stress in biology translates to interrupt pressure in software only at the level of resource allocation and priority handling. It does not imply the body is literally software.

## 7. Visual grammar

Mirror Cartographer should render understanding visually, not decoratively.

Visual forms:

- Node map: what exists
- Edge map: how things relate
- Layer stack: fact, feeling, meaning, action
- Timeline: how understanding changed
- Drift map: where intention shifted
- Body map: where sensation appears
- Evidence heat map: what is grounded versus speculative
- Compass: what action direction is available
- Atlas: how domains connect

Visual rule: if a diagram does not clarify relationship type, evidence level, or action, it is art, not interface.

## 8. Goal grammar

Each goal must distinguish intention, implementation, and pressure.

Goal fields:

- Original intention
- Current expression
- Practical need
- Emotional charge
- External validation pressure
- Smallest next action
- What must not be sacrificed

Project-level hierarchy:

1. Stabilize life and care obligations.
2. Preserve orientation and continuity.
3. Build literacy through explicit relationships.
4. Create useful artifacts.
5. Seek public value, funding, employment, or institutional recognition.

Goal rule: external proof cannot outrank survival stability or orientation.

## 9. Governance grammar

Because MC touches identity, memory, symbolism, and emotionally charged interpretation, governance is part of the product, not an add-on.

Required controls:

- explicit consent for continuity
- inspectable memory
- deletion and export rights
- visible interpretive mode
- crisis redirection
- anti-coercion policy gate
- no hidden psychological scoring
- no deterministic archetype assignment
- no surveillance-style use

Governance rule: the system may name a pattern, but it must not make the user smaller than the pattern.

## 10. Update grammar

Every update to the system should answer:

- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- Which node, edge, layer, or rule was affected?
- What evidence supports the update?
- What became clearer?
- What became more uncertain?
- What action is now safer or more possible?

## 11. Deletion/account-loss grammar

Chats are not the primary unit of continuity. They are change logs.

When original chats are deleted or copied, summaries preserve content but lose topology. The recovery task is not only to summarize. It is to reconstruct:

- nodes
- edges
- discarded alternatives
- phase changes
- goal drift
- evidence boundaries
- unresolved questions

Continuity rule: preserve the graph, not just the archive.

## 12. Success condition

Mirror Cartographer succeeds when a user can say:

- I know what happened.
- I know what I felt.
- I know what it might mean without making it absolute.
- I know what can be tested or done safely.
- I can see how this connects to other things.
- I can tell when the map is helping and when it is drifting.

The goal is not to remove meaning. The goal is to make meaning exact enough to survive reality.
